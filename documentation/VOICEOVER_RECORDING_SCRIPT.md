## Part 2: WHY (0:45 – 1:45)

### Scene 4 (0:45 – 1:05)
For more than six decades, astronauts have pushed human exploration farther from Earth. But spaceflight also places the human body under continuous stress.

NASA research shows that in microgravity, astronauts can lose approximately 1 to 1.5% of bone mineral density every month during spaceflight. Up to two liters of body fluid shifts from the lower body toward the head when astronauts enter weightlessness. NASA also reports that 70% of astronauts on long-duration space missions experience signs of Spaceflight-Associated Neuro-ocular Syndrome, or SANS—involving optic nerve sheath swelling and structural brain alterations.

This concern led us to embrace the official challenge: "Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions," in NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026.

---

### Scene 5 (1:05 – 1:20)
In deep space, distance changes everything. An astronaut’s body is constantly adapting, and every heartbeat, every micro-shift, every warning matters.

On long-duration missions, astronauts may be the first to notice that something is changing, understand what it means, and decide what to do next. And a missed warning can affect not only one astronaut, but the safety and success of the entire mission.

---

### Scene 6 (1:20 – 1:35)
Health information shouldn’t be scattered or hard to access when an astronaut needs it most. On Earth, mission control needs the complete picture. Inside the spacecraft, astronauts need their daily routine and self-check clarity. And outside during a spacewalk, they may have only their helmet and their voice.

---

### Scene 7 (1:35 – 1:45)
So we asked a simple question: What if an astronaut’s health could follow them—wherever they are?

Not just monitored from Earth. Not just stored in a distant database. But available directly to the crew member who needs it, exactly when they need it?

## Part 3: WHAT (1:45 – 2:55)

### Scene 8 (1:45 – 1:51)
Our answer is HELIOS.-Health Evaluation Logistic Intelligent Onboard System. A unified platform designed to help astronauts monitor, understand, and respond to their health throughout deep-space missions.

---

### Scene 9 (1:51 – 2:00)
HELIOS combines NASA Open Science Data Repository datasets(OSDR) from the SpaceX Inspiration mission with simulated real-time spaceflight telemetry.

We integrate OSD-569 for whole-blood and hematological baselines, and OSD-575 for metabolic, cardiovascular, and immune biomarkers.

---

### Scene 10 (2:00 – 2:10)
The Dashboard brings the mission's complete health picture into one view—crew vitals, hardware-decoupled 90 FPS ECG waveforms, and ECLSS life-support telemetry.

At its center is the 3D Holographic Bio-Twin—allowing the crew to rotate and inspect anatomical subsystems, like cardiac dynamics, bone mineral density, and optic nerve pressure swelling, in real time.

---

### Scene 11 (2:10 – 2:20)
Because astronauts need proactive health management, HELIOS introduces two essential daily tools: Self-Check and Mission Routine.

In Self-Check, crew members log subjective symptoms, pain locations, and mood indices that cross-correlate with biosensors. And in Routine, daily schedules dynamically adapt to biometric fatigue—countering microgravity bone loss with adaptive ARED resistance protocols, alongside a validated Psychomotor Vigilance Task to measure cognitive reaction time down to the millisecond.

---

### Scene 12 (2:20 – 2:30)
Our Astronaut Health Telemetry System organizes measurements into ten physiological categories—linking every biomarker, from cosmic radiation dosimetry to bone resorption markers, into actionable health insight under real spaceflight conditions.

---

### Scene 13 (2:30 – 2:42)
When an in-flight crisis occurs, HELIOS acts instantly.

Watch our simulator,trigger a deep-space Solar Radiation Storm: radiation flux surges to 85 milligrays per hour, alarms flash crimson, and the onboard Helix-our Ai Analyst, commands the crew into the water-shielded storm shelter.

Simultaneously, for chronic hazards like zero-g Bone Loss, HELIOS tracks bone mineral density declines on the 3D interactive Human Body, automatically prescribing adaptive resistance countermeasures in the Mission Routine.

---

### Scene 14 (2:42 – 2:48)
At Earth Mission Control, flight teams monitor the same synchronized data—equipped with a physical speed-of-light delay engine that simulates communication lag from low Earth orbit to Mars solar conjunction.

---

### Scene 15 (2:48 – 2:55)
Outside the ship during spacewalks, astronauts cannot hold a tablet.

Our innovation is the HELIOS Suit HUD—projecting health metrics, suit life support, oxygen reserves, and tactical navigation directly onto the helmet visor. Hands-free, immediate, wherever they go.

## Part 4: SO WHAT NEXT & IMPACT (2:55 – 4:00)

### Scene 16 (2:55 – 3:15)
HELIOS was never only about space. Its core power is the architecture: taking fragmented biosignals, detecting meaningful changes, and recommending immediate action.

On Earth, this same intelligence can protect firefighters inside burning buildings, or assist healthcare workers in remote rural clinics cut off from specialist hospitals.

---

### Scene 17 (3:15 – 3:35)
We scale the intelligence, not just the hardware.

Inside our interactive 3D ISS Deck, astronauts can navigate 10 station modules and check bulkhead telemetry. From commercial orbital flights to Lunar Gateway and permanent Mars habitats, HELIOS expands seamlessly across larger crews and new sensor arrays.

---

HELIOS turns raw signals into awareness, understanding, and life-saving action.
